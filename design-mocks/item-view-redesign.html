Settled

Decisions from the 17 Aug pass, recorded here so the build doesn't relitigate them.

  1. The plant score is a set. Per item: a list of distinct plants. Per week: the union. Garlic in two meals counts once.
  2. Target is 30 per week — shown as a 20–30 “good” band after reading the source, since that's the documentary's actual wording.
  3. Only recipes score. Eating out is surfaced but unscored, and a week of it will simply read low for now. Framing stays neutral — no red, no zero, no scolding. Refinement deferred.
  4. Bottom drawer is the standard home for extra actions — everywhere, including the ⋮ menu.
  5. “Auto create item on plan” = PLA-52.
  6. Add the new fields and backfill. Two users, little data, so re-processing existing items is safe.
  7. Prep / cook / servings stay, demoted to one quiet line at the bottom (pin 8 on screen B).
  8. Seats is a built-in ticket field, not a custom section.
  9. Tags come off the item page. Still associated under the hood. The structured attributes are the intended replacement as the filter dimension, but that's a later ticket.
  10. Attributes render as chips — locked in. Extending the pattern to other item types is deliberately left for later.
  11. PLA-35 (food pyramid) is superseded by this score. The plant-group breakdown on screen C is plant categories only — no protein or dairy tiers.

Resolved on the second pass

  1. The Nature paper is a critique, not a source. Hey & Zurawski, Humanities and Social Sciences Communications, Jan 2026 — a media-studies reading of the documentary, arguing it uses microbes as a “decoy” that shifts systemic food-access problems onto individual responsibility. Nothing to implement from it, but two things to heed: the documentary's real wording is “between 20 and 30 is considered good” (so the ring now shows a band, not a pass mark), and its “always be counting” framing is exactly the tone to avoid.
  2. One plant = one plant source. Extraction emits the source organism alongside the display name, and the set dedupes on that. Egg noodles, bread and flour all resolve to wheat and count once. Soy sauce resolves to two plants, soybean and wheat. Side effect: red and yellow onion merge into one — arguably right, since two onion colours aren't dietary diversity.
  3. Everything is an enum. effort: easy | medium | hard — deliberately the same three values as the existing DRINK_DIFFICULTIES, so drinks and recipes share one scale. spice: none | mild | medium | hot. Plant category: vegetable | fruit | whole_grain | legume | nut | seed.
  4. Herbs and spices don't count. Matches the dietitian article backing PLA-56, and stops a spice cabinet carrying a third of the weekly target. Dropped from the enum and the breakdown — the sample week fell from 18 to 16 as a result.
  5. Backfill re-processes everything, no skip branch for hand-edited items.
  6. Event attributes logged as a follow-up ticket, not built here.

Nothing blocking

Every question raised across the three passes is answered. The remaining unknowns are build-time details — how the extraction prompt phrases the plant-source request, and whether the backfill runs as one job or per-item — neither of which changes anything on these screens.

One flagged consequence: normalising to plant source merges red and yellow onion into a single plant, which reverses the earlier “each one is distinct” call. It's the same mechanism that fixes wheat, so the two can't be separated without a special case. Worth a nod before it's built.
